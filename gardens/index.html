🌐 Regional Food & Agriculture Organizations

Connect directly with local university programs, non-profits, and extension offices supporting urban agriculture across the Valley:

UTRGV Agroecology Program ↗ Manages The Hub of Prosperity, certified organic campus gardens, farmer apprenticeships, and regional food summits in Edinburg. Brownsville Wellness Coalition ↗ Operates 6+ community gardens in Brownsville (Tres Angeles, Esperanza, Lemon Grass, etc.), cooking classes (La Cocina Alegre), and the Cannery Market. Texas A&M AgriLife (Hidalgo County) ↗ Hidalgo County Master Gardeners, soil testing, demonstration gardens at N. San Juan Community Center, and Master Gardener intern training. Texas A&M AgriLife (Cameron County) ↗ Cameron County Master Gardener Demonstration Arboretum in San Benito, native plant propagation, and home vegetable guides. National Butterfly Center (Mission) ↗ 100-acre botanical preserve with over 200 native host and nectar species, nursery, and conservation workshops. Valley Nature Center (Weslaco) ↗ 6-acre historic native plant preserve, cactus rockeries, butterfly feeding stations, and native plant nursery sales. Quinta Mazatlan Urban Sanctuary ↗ 20-acre urban thornforest sanctuary, Center for Urban Ecology, native ethnobotanical trails, and eco-workshops in McAllen. Native Plant Society of Texas (RGV) ↗ Resources on drought-tolerant landscaping, native thornscrub flora, and community pollinator habitats.

🤝 How to Join or Volunteer at an RGV Community Garden

  1. Find a garden near you: Use the Interactive Map or search by your city.
  2. Select your involvement level:
    • Adopt a Bed / Plot Rental: Available at McAllen Community Garden, UTRGV Agroecology (Edinburg), and Brownsville Wellness Coalition locations. Most charge a nominal fee or require attendance at free community gardening classes.
    • Volunteer Workdays: Open to all! Weekly opportunities at The Hub of Prosperity (Saturdays 8 AM–12 PM), Hidalgo Master Gardeners (Tue/Thu 8 AM–12 PM), and Terra Preta Farm.
  3. Supplies provided: Most community gardens provide city water/drip systems, compost, basic shovels, and trowels. Bring your own sun protection, garden gloves, and water bottle.

🍓 South Texas U-Pick Farms & Orchard Etiquette

While public pick-your-own operations are rare in the RGV due to strict citrus agricultural quarantines (Mexican fruit fly monitoring), select family farms offer extraordinary seasonal picking experiences:

🌟 Top RGV U-Pick & Farm Experiences:
  • Obst Family Farm (Alamo, TX): Famous Rio Red grapefruit, oranges (Oct–Feb), sweet onions and vegetables (Nov–Jan), watermelons and cantaloupes (Apr–May). Always call ahead at (956) 787-6917 to confirm field availability.
  • Yahweh's All-Natural Farm (Harlingen, TX): Certified organic "Pick-Your-Own Carrot Days", organic greens, farm walks, and CSA pickup. Call (956) 412-4916 for upcoming picking weekends.
  • Bells Farm to Market (McAllen, TX): Fresh field harvests, sweet corn, citrus, and volunteer harvesting assistance.

⚠️ Pro-Tips for Visiting:

  • Always Call Before You Go: Harvest availability fluctuates rapidly with South Texas weather and crop ripening.
  • Timing: Arrive early in the morning (8:00 AM – 10:30 AM) to beat the South Texas afternoon heat.
  • Containers: Bring reusable crates, breathable bags, or sturdy boxes for your harvest.

📅 Rio Grande Valley Vegetable Planting Calendar (Zone 9b / 10a)

Unlike northern regions, the Rio Grande Valley has two prime growing seasons: a warm Spring season and a mild, highly productive Fall/Winter season.

Crop Fall / Winter Season Spring / Early Summer Season Tips for RGV Soils
Tomatoes Plant transplants in Aug – Sep Plant transplants Jan 15 – Feb 28 Use heat-tolerant varieties (Tycoon, Celebrity, Solar Fire)
Peppers (Jalapeño, Serrano, Bell) Aug 1 – Sep 15 Jan 15 – Mar 15 Thrives in RGV heat; mulch heavily to conserve moisture
Carrots & Beets Oct 1 – Dec 15 Jan 1 – Feb 15 Loose, composted sandy loam prevents root branching
Leafy Greens (Kale, Chard, Lettuce) Sep 15 – Nov 30 Jan 1 – Feb 28 Peak season is Nov–Feb; shade cloth extends spring harvest
Cucumbers & Squash (Calabacita) Aug 15 – Sep 30 Feb 15 – Apr 1 Watch for squash vine borers; pollinator plants increase fruit set
Citrus (Grapefruit, Oranges, Limes) Harvest Oct – Feb Fertilize early spring flush Rio Red Grapefruit is the iconic Texas state fruit
Herbs (Cilantro, Oregano, Basil) Oct 1 – Dec 1 (Cilantro/Parsley) Mar 1 – May 1 (Basil, Mint) Cilantro bolts quickly in heat; grow in cool winter months
