# Statewide Silverleaf Nightshade Collection Expedition
**Duration:** 6 Days / 5 Nights
**Scope:** Rio Grande Valley → Central/West Texas → Panhandle → Austin → Houston → Gulf Coast → Rio Grande Valley
**Purpose:** To systematically sample *Solanum elaeagnifolium* (Silverleaf Nightshade) across diverse soil types, climatic zones, and ecosystems to assess plant phenology, insect herbivore associations (specifically *Leptinotarsa texana*), and localized fecundity metrics.

---

## Part 1: Roadside Sampling Methodology

To ensure data uniformity and maximize safety along Texas highways and rural roads, the following protocols must be adhered to at every collection site.

### 1.1 Safety Protocol
*   **Vehicle Placement:** Always pull completely off the road onto the shoulder or a designated turnout. Ensure hazard lights are active.
*   **Visibility:** All team members must wear ANSI-certified high-visibility safety vests when outside the vehicle.
*   **Awareness:** Face oncoming traffic when sampling near the roadside. Never cross an active multi-lane highway on foot to sample plants on the opposite side.

### 1.2 Site Selection Strategy
*   **Frequency:** Attempt to sample at least once every 50-75 miles, or whenever a noticeable shift in soil type or ecosystem occurs (e.g., crossing from the Edwards Plateau into the Rolling Plains).
*   **Minimum Distance:** Ensure sites are at least 5-10 miles apart to capture distinct subpopulations and avoid sampling rhizomal clones of the same genet.

### 1.3 Data Collection Procedure
At each site, utilize the **Digital Data Collection App** to instantly sync data to the central Google Sheet:
1.  **GPS & Location:** Log coordinates immediately upon exiting the vehicle.
2.  **Environmental Metrics:** Record current temperature and visually assess soil/drought stress.
3.  **Plant Metrics:** Randomly select 5-10 plants (spaced 5m apart). Record plant height, damage score, and phenological stage.
4.  **Entomological Metrics:** Conduct visual counts of adult insects. Critically, record **fecundity estimates** (egg masses or larval clusters) to assess biocontrol potential.
5.  **Physical Vouchers:** If an unidentified insect or unique plant phenotype is found, collect a physical specimen in a labeled vial (Date, Site ID, GPS).

## Part 2: 6-Day Expedition Itinerary

*This itinerary is designed to balance driving endurance with sufficient daylight hours required for rigorous field sampling.*

### Day 1: The Western Push
**Route:** Hidalgo County → Laredo → San Angelo
**Distance:** ~400 miles (approx. 6.5 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Assess populations transitioning from the subtropical Rio Grande Valley into the semi-arid brush country and the southern edge of the Edwards Plateau.
*   **Morning:** Depart Hidalgo County; sample along US-83 N towards Laredo.
*   **Afternoon:** Transition to US-277 N through Del Rio and Sonora, monitoring for drought-stressed populations.
*   **Overnight Hub:** **San Angelo, TX**

### Day 2: The Panhandle Climb
**Route:** San Angelo → Midland/Odessa → Lubbock
**Distance:** ~200 - 250 miles (approx. 3.5 - 4 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Investigate the impact of intensive agriculture and the High Plains ecosystem on SLN fitness and herbivore presence.
*   **Morning:** Sample north along US-87 or TX-137 through oilfield territories and agricultural plains.
*   **Afternoon:** Frequent stops in the southern Panhandle where soil types shift dramatically to sandy loams.
*   **Overnight Hub:** **Lubbock, TX**

### Day 3: The Central Descent
**Route:** Lubbock → Abilene → Austin
**Distance:** ~375 miles (approx. 6 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Track the phenotypic shifts as the elevation drops and the climate becomes more humid transitioning into Central Texas (Hill Country).
*   **Morning:** Head southeast via US-84 E and US-277 S.
*   **Afternoon:** Sample heavily through the rocky, limestone-rich soils of the Hill Country before reaching the urban interface.
*   **Overnight Hub:** **Austin, TX**

### Day 4: The Eastern Leg
**Route:** Austin → Houston
**Distance:** ~165 miles (approx. 2.5 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Study urban and highly disturbed sub-populations. Assess how heavy anthropogenic disturbance (mowing, construction) impacts defense traits (spines).
*   **Morning:** Extensive sampling on the outskirts of Austin and along the US-290 E corridor.
*   **Afternoon:** Arrive in the humid, clay-heavy soils of the Gulf Coastal Plain.
*   **Overnight Hub:** **Houston, TX**

### Day 5: The Coastal Drop
**Route:** Houston → Victoria → Corpus Christi
**Distance:** ~210 miles (approx. 3.5 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Evaluate populations in the humid coastal prairies. Heavy focus on comparing these plants to the arid West Texas plants sampled on Days 1-2.
*   **Morning:** Follow US-59 S / I-69 towards Victoria.
*   **Afternoon:** Transition to US-77 S towards the coast, taking time to search for pollinator interactions in this distinct biome.
*   **Overnight Hub:** **Corpus Christi, TX**

### Day 6: The Southern Return
**Route:** Corpus Christi → Brownsville → Hidalgo County
**Distance:** ~200 miles (approx. 3.5 hours of driving)
**Goals:** Final sampling through the sandy soils of the Wild Horse Desert (Kenedy/Brooks counties) before returning to the home range.
*   **Morning:** Sample south along US-77 through the King Ranch territory.
*   **Afternoon:** Cut west from Brownsville back into Hidalgo County to conclude the loop.
*   **End of Expedition.**
